## REF-1 — `Moonchild Tarot Card The Star.png`

**Type:** Illustration — tarot card  
**Source:** Moonchild Tarot deck, "The Star" (card XVII)

**Key properties observed:**
- Portrait card format with ~85% image field; ~10% ruled footer name-bar
- Footer contains two elements only: a Roman numeral index and the card name in spaced serif capitals with a period
- No body copy, no metadata, no supporting labels
- A single high-chroma warm accent (golden star) against a muted cool-dark ground; all other elements are restrained
- The thin visible border is a completion signal, not a decoration: it says "this is one finished thing"
- Focal hierarchy created by a single luminosity event (the star), not by containment or arrows

**Relevance to Movie Mood:** The "one complete world + name below" structural attitude. Each film could be treated as a complete world worth entering, not as a filtered result in a collection. This maps to Full Reveal and Tonight's Pick.

## REF-2 — `136937644917437380.png`

**Type:** Illustration — tarot card  
**Source:** Moonchild Tarot deck, "L'Intuition"

**Key properties observed:**
- Same portrait card format as REF-1; denser composition but still reads as one unified scene
- Solar halo creates a strong radial focal point within dense layering
- Recurring visual symbols (black cats) appear across both tarot references — these are vocabulary elements, not arbitrary decoration
- Footer: one identification label with flanking ornamental marks; nothing else
- Warm golden-teal palette with warm candlelight as secondary light source

**Relevance to Movie Mood:** Reinforces the "symbolic density within a contained frame" principle. Confirms the name-below, world-above structural attitude. Also: density and richness are possible within a single coherent composition if there is one clear focal event.

## REF-3 — `UX and web design for street photographer.png`

**Type:** Web design mockup  
**Source:** Francesco Gioia street photographer portfolio

**Key properties observed:**
- Full-bleed pure-black ground; all color comes from the photographs; the UI itself carries no chromatic energy
- Enormous condensed white sans-serif type at near-full viewport width — type as spatial object, not heading
- Binary type system: display scale (gigantic) and metadata scale (near-invisible); nothing between
- Photographs are not contained — they fragment, bleed, cut across type at angles
- Gallery state: horizontal contact-sheet strip without gutters; butted photographs as a unit
- "08 / GALLERY" footer: numerals and word as typographic objects at equal scale in opposite corners — typography as layout
- Navigation is whisper-small in corners and essentially invisible

**Relevance to Movie Mood:** Pattern A (image as atmosphere source) at its most extreme. Pattern B (type as spatial object). Pattern C (no card containers). The contact-sheet strip is a spatial "strip of frames" idea separable from any film-strip motif.

## REF-5 — `649925790022103006.jpeg`

**Type:** Web design mockup  
**Source:** "The Late Checkout" — travel website (Figma/Dribbble origin)

**Key properties observed:**
- Cream/warm white ground; one warm red accent (not pure red — has orange); photography supplies all other warmth
- Display serif headline set directly over a landscape photograph — large, at sufficient contrast, not confined to a safe corner
- Script/handwritten inscription as a light supplementary element at image edge
- One-accent-color system applied consistently to navigation, headline, and footer
- Grid state: asymmetric (one dominant center image flanked by smaller strips)
- Story titles beneath each image ("A heartbreak-healing solo trip to the Greek Islands") — story/feeling leads, not category/genre
- Pairing of enormous serif title + very small sans tagline as recurring editorial device
- Individual images have no radius, no shadow, no container

**Relevance to Movie Mood:** Pattern D (single accent). Pattern G (story over taxonomy). Asymmetric grid as a spatial principle applicable to the three-slate. Editorial lead (vibeSummary/curiosityHook) over metadata. The macro-level card frame is interesting as a "complete thing" signal but not as an individual content container.

## REF-6 — `Stunning Figma Website Mockup for 2025.jpeg`

**Type:** Web design mockup  
**Source:** Frank Ponce / "Creative Consigliere for Empire Builders" agency site

**Key properties observed:**
- Dark background, heavy condensed orange/white type, film-strip motifs with Kodak branding, full-bleed photography, scrolling marquee text, pricing grids
- The loudest and most conventional reference in the set — fits the "dark agency/creator brand" visual category
- Film-strip element creates horizontal rhythm of contained frames (spatial idea separable from the motif)
- Scrolling marquee text used as a transition element — typography as texture, not content

**Relevance to Movie Mood:** Mostly as a negative reference. Almost every aesthetic decision contradicts Movie Mood's quiet certainty. The film-strip motif is rejected. The agency/hustle DNA is rejected. The spatial strip-of-frames idea is separable and worth noting.

## REF-7 — `The Bolshoi Theatre - redesign.jpeg`

**Type:** Web design mockup  
**Source:** Bolshoi Theatre website redesign (Dribbble/Behance origin)

**Key properties observed:**
- Near-black ground; off-white text; single crimson accent (the crimson of stage lighting and theater seats — not orange, not pink)
- Performance photographs at full-width with no hard crop edge — images blend/dissolve into the dark background through vignette; performers emerge from darkness
- Large editorial pull-quotes: large enough to be a layout event, not a text block
- Crimson accent: italic word inserted mid-phrase in a white-serif sentence — one word, highlighted by color change alone, no enclosure, no bolding
- Crimson used exactly once per view section in a meaningful role; never decoratively
- No card containers anywhere. No borders on images. No radius. Hierarchy through scale and position only.
- Cast table and schedule rows are the most compact, visually restrained elements on the page
- Schedule table: one row highlighted in crimson (next performance date) — informational, not celebratory

**Relevance to Movie Mood:** The strongest web reference in the set. Direct mapping to Full Reveal (production detail page = film detail page). Pattern A (image as atmosphere). Pattern B (pull-quote as spatial event). Pattern C (no containers). Pattern D (one accent, one role). Pattern F (image dissolves into ground — film as light source). The single inline accent approach (crimson italic word) is directly applicable to `whyWatch`/`curiosityHook` editorial copy.

## REF-8 — `Work _ Charlotte Evans.jpeg`

**Type:** Web design  
**Source:** Charlotte Evans — film director portfolio

**Key properties observed:**
- Very light periwinkle/blue-gray background — a specific temperature, not neutral gray, not white
- Cold-ground / warm-accent system: light periwinkle + one warm orange-red accent
- Display headline "WORK." in large high-contrast serif — includes the period, which signals completion, not invitation
- Two typographic registers only: display serif and micro tracked caps
- 2-column image grid: full-bleed landscape stills, no gutter, no radius, no shadow — images sit directly on the ground
- Category tag ("MUSIC VIDEO") in micro caps above title; title in warm red serif below the image
- Three filter pills: thin outline, minimal radius — restrained
- Gallery logic: photographs on a ground, named below, categorized above

**Relevance to Movie Mood:** Pattern C (no containers on individual content items). Pattern D (single accent). The name-below-image structure is directly applicable to Glimpse. The periwinkle ground demonstrates that the ground can have a specific temperature without asserting a "theme." The period in "WORK." is a small example of punctuation as a completion signal.

## Summary — What to Check Before Starting Visual Work

1. Is the ground chromatically quiet, leaving the artwork to supply atmosphere? (REF-1, REF-2, REF-3, REF-7, REF-8)
2. Does the primary type element function as a spatial object, not just a heading? (REF-3, REF-5, REF-7, REF-8)
3. Is there one accent, used exactly once per surface in a specific semantic role? (REF-5, REF-7, REF-8)
4. Is editorial copy (`curiosityHook`, `vibeSummary`) visually primary over metadata? (REF-5, REF-7)
5. Does the backdrop dissolve into the ground rather than sitting in a container? (REF-7)
6. Are individual content items on the ground without card containers? (REF-3, REF-7, REF-8)
7. Is the emotional register warm and grounded, not cold-premium? (REF-4, REF-9)
8. Have you run the Composition Workflow (Section 5 of SKILL.md) before generating anything?
